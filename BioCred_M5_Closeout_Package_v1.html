1Try it yourself

The same two test logins you already have: one reviewer, one biologist. Nothing has changed about them.

Open the demo
  1. Open the directory4 min

    No login. Profiles are listed A to Z. Type a word, or filter by region, discipline, experience domain or years, and the order switches to relevance to what you typed. Each card shows the small five-domain pentagon.

  2. Open a profile, then press “View Experience Map”4 min

    Read the page once with the map off and once with it on. Under the map: “Experience Summary · Derived from verified documentation”.

  3. Open PEND011 min

    It must refuse. It is waiting for review, so it should not exist publicly, in the directory or anywhere else. (PEND99 used to play this role, but it was marked partial on 14 October and is now public. That decision was left as it was.)

  4. Sign in as the reviewerreviewer6 min

    The queue, a submission, a document through a five-minute link, the decision record. A profile that was rejected and sent back carries a resubmitted tag, with both sides of the exchange in its history.

  5. Follow the operator guide on your ownoptional · 10 min

    Part of the test is that the guide is enough for you to run every reviewer step without asking me anything. If at any point it isn't, that is a defect in the guide and I'd like to know.

If a page looks broken: staging is on the free plan and sleeps after a quiet stretch. The first visit can fail until it wakes. Nothing is lost. Just tell me and I'll wake it.

2The test we agreed, answered

On 13 September I proposed four sentences as the test of “done”, so you could judge the milestone against something agreed in advance rather than an impression.

#The testWhere you see it
1A person who has never seen the system signs up, builds a profile, submits it, and is reviewed by a reviewer who is not me. The profile then appears publicly with its experience map.Signup, intake and submission are the M2 path. Review uses the reviewer account, which is not mine. Approval publishes the profile with its map. New accounts sign up on their own, and a rejected person can now correct and resubmit.
2A second person finds them through search, filtering on an experience domain and a region, without knowing their registry ID.Step 1 of the demo. Domain and region filters are both on the directory, and results link straight to the profile.
3A profile that has not been approved is invisible to the public page and absent from every search result.Step 3 of the demo, and the security checks in section 5: the public views return only approved profiles, and the underlying tables refuse anonymous reads outright.
4The operator guide is enough for you to run every reviewer step yourself without asking me anything.Step 5 of the demo. This one only you can confirm.

3What Milestone 5 added

PieceWhat it does
Directory and searchA public directory of approved profiles. Text search across name, title, organisation, discipline, species, habitats and credentials. Filters for region, discipline, experience domain and minimum years. A to Z when nobody is searching, relevance to the words and filters actually used once they are. Relevance never reads the experience map.
Result cardsEach card carries the five-domain pentagon. Every pentagon is the same size and only its shape varies, so no profile can look “bigger” or better than another.
Correction after rejectionA rejected biologist sees the reviewer's written reason, corrects the profile and sends it back. They can't resend without saying what changed. Records already verified stay verified.
The submit rule, in the databaseSubmission is now refused from any state other than draft or rejected. Before, an approved profile could be pulled back into the queue by pressing a button. The button is gone, and the rule holds even if someone bypasses the page.
Document paths closedFound in my September adversarial pass: the storage path of each uploaded document (which, with real users, would carry a person's name and qualification in the filename) was readable anonymously. The files were always shut. Now the paths are too.
Your notes from 15 October“Experience Summary · Derived from verified documentation”; no “sparse” or “limited”; “Documented experience concentrated in…” instead of titles like Specialist; A to Z by default; “Applicant Attestation”; Sign in / Create account as tabs; no centroid line on the public map; stronger green contrast.
DocumentationThe Operator Guide (for you), the Participant Guide (for the first pilot users), the walkthrough page, and this package.

7Known limitations

Stated plainly, so you hear them from me first. None of them is one of the major defects in Exhibit A.

LimitationWhat it means
One decision per submissionA reviewer can't approve two credentials and reject a third in the same pass.
No reopening a verification yetOnce verified, a record can't be put back under review from the reviewer page. This is your “Reopen Verification” idea, on the production list.
File type checked by extensionA file named as a PDF is trusted to be one. Checking the content is production hardening.
Publishing the map is run by handIn production it would run on a schedule, so a public profile could take a few minutes to appear after approval.
Staging sleepsFree plan. The first visit after a quiet period can fail until it wakes.
Search runs in the browserRight for a pilot of tens of profiles. With hundreds it should move into the database.
Library version not pinnedsupabase-js loads as “latest v2”. Production should fix an exact version.
Styles repeated page by pageThe palette is consistent, but each page carries its own copy. Worth bringing together before anyone else touches the code.
No production environmentYour domain, a paid database, email confirmation, scheduled publishing and backups are all production work.
