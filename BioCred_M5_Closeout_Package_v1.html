2. The demo: how to walk it

Start here

https://biocred-staging-demo.netlify.app

The same two test logins you already have: one reviewer, one biologist. Nothing has changed about them.

  1. Open the directory · 4 min
    No login. Profiles are listed A to Z. Type a word, or filter by region, discipline, experience domain or years, and the order switches to relevance to what you typed. Each card carries the small five-domain pentagon.
  2. Open any profile from the results, then press “View Experience Map” · 4 min
    Read the page once with the map off and once with it on. Under the map: “Experience Summary · Derived from verified documentation”.
  3. Open PEND01 · 1 min
    It must refuse. It is a profile that is waiting for review, and it should not exist publicly, in the directory or anywhere else. (PEND99, which used to play this role, was marked partial on 14 October and is now public. That decision was left as it was.)
  4. Sign in as the reviewer · 6 min · reviewer account
    The queue, a submission, a document through a five-minute link, the decision record. A profile that was rejected and sent back carries a resubmitted tag, with both sides of the exchange in its history.
  5. Optional: follow the operator guide on your own · 10 min
    Part of the test is that the guide is enough for you to run every reviewer step without asking me anything. If at any point it is not, that is a defect in the guide and I would like to know.
If a page looks broken: staging is on the free plan and sleeps after a quiet stretch. The first visit can fail until it wakes. Nothing is lost. Tell me and I will wake it.

3. The agreed test, answered

In the M5 scope note of 13 September I proposed four sentences as the test of “done”, so you could judge the milestone against something agreed in advance rather than against an impression. Here they are, with where you can see each one.

#The testWhere you see it
1A person who has never seen the system signs up, builds a profile, submits it, and is reviewed by a reviewer who is not me. Their profile then appears publicly with its experience map.Signup, intake and submission are the M2 path. Review is the reviewer account, which is not mine. Approval publishes the profile with its map (M4). New accounts can sign up on their own, and a rejected person can now correct and resubmit (section 4).
2A second person finds them through search, by filtering on an experience domain and a region, without knowing their registry ID.Step 1 of the demo. Domain and region filters are both on the directory page, and results link straight to the profile.
3A profile that has not been approved is invisible to the public page and absent from every search result.Step 3 of the demo, and the security run in section 6: the public views return only approved profiles, and the underlying tables refuse anonymous reads outright.
4The operator guide is enough for you to run every reviewer step yourself without asking me anything.Step 5 of the demo. This one only you can confirm.

4. What Milestone 5 added

PieceWhat it does
Directory and searchA public directory of approved profiles. Text search across name, title, organisation, discipline, species, habitats and credentials. Filters for region, discipline, experience domain and minimum years. A to Z when nobody is searching; relevance to the words and filters actually used once they are. Relevance never reads the experience map.
Result cardsEach card carries the five-domain pentagon. Every pentagon has the same size and only its shape varies, so no profile can look “bigger” or better than another.
Correction after rejectionA rejected biologist sees the reviewer's written reason, corrects the profile and sends it back. They cannot resend without saying what changed. Records already verified stay verified.
The submit rule moved into the databaseSubmission is now refused from any state other than draft or rejected. Before this, an approved profile could be pulled back into the queue by pressing a button. The button is gone, and the database rule stays in place even if someone gets past the page.
Document paths closed to the publicFound during the adversarial pass in September: the storage path of each uploaded document (which, with real users, would carry a person's name and qualification in the filename) was readable anonymously. The files were always shut. The paths now are too.
Your notes from 15 October“Experience Summary · Derived from verified documentation”; no “sparse” or “limited”; “Documented experience concentrated in…” instead of titles like Specialist; A to Z by default; “Applicant Attestation”; Sign in / Create account shown as tabs; centroid line hidden on the public map; stronger green contrast.
DocumentationThe Operator Guide (for you), the Participant Guide (for the first pilot users), the walkthrough page, and this package.

8. Known limitations

As before, stated plainly so you hear them from me first. None of them is one of the major defects in Exhibit A.

LimitationWhat it means
One decision per submissionA reviewer cannot approve two credentials and reject a third in the same pass.
No reopening of a verification yetOnce verified, a record cannot be put back under review from the reviewer page. This is the “Reopen Verification” idea from your October note, on the production list.
File type checked by extensionA file named as a PDF is trusted to be one. Checking the content is production hardening.
Publishing is a job, run by hand on stagingIn production it would run on a schedule, so a public profile could take a few minutes to appear after approval.
Staging sleepsFree plan. The first visit after a quiet period can fail until it wakes.
Search runs in the browserRight for a pilot of tens of profiles. With hundreds or more it should move to the database.
Library version not pinnedsupabase-js is loaded as “latest v2”. Production should fix an exact version.
Colours defined page by pageThe palette is consistent, but each page carries its own copy. Bringing it together in one place is worth doing before anyone else touches the code.
No production environmentYour domain, a paid database, email confirmation, scheduled publishing and backups are all production work.
