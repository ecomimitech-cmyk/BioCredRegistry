1. What Milestone 5 is

The agreement calls it the Functional Pilot: the registry usable privately, end to end, by people who are not us — with search and filtering, quality checking, and the documentation to run it. And it is explicit that mockups, static pages and disconnected demos do not count.

In practical terms, everything you clicked through this week proves the chain works for one person at a time, if you already know their registry ID. A registry nobody can search is an archive. Milestone 5 is what turns it into something a person can actually use to find someone.

Where this leaves the schedule. With Milestones 1 to 4 accepted, four of the five are done. Milestone 5 is the last one, and it is also the one the Functional Pilot Completion document depends on.

2. What “done” will mean

This is the acceptance test. I would rather you judged the milestone against a written test we agreed beforehand than against an impression afterwards.

A person who has never seen the system signs up, builds a profile, submits it, and is reviewed by a reviewer who is not me. Their profile then appears publicly with its experience map.

A second person finds them through search — by filtering on an experience domain and a region, without knowing their registry ID.

A profile that has not been approved is invisible to the public page and absent from every search result.

And the operator guide is enough for you to run every reviewer step yourself without asking me anything.

If any of those four is not true, Milestone 5 is not done and I would not ask you to accept it.

3. What I will build

Search and filtering — the genuinely new part

There is no search anywhere in the system today. Not a weak one: none. So this is the only part of the project so far that is built from nothing rather than wired together.

PieceWhat it does
A directoryA page listing published profiles, instead of needing a registry ID to see anyone.
Text searchName, title, organisation, discipline.
FiltersRegion, discipline, years of experience, and the five experience domains.
Search by where the experience actually sits“Show me people whose experience is concentrated in Regulation & Policy.” This uses the same analysis that draws the experience map, so it searches on what somebody has documented rather than on what they happened to type. It is the part of this product that would be hardest for anyone else to copy.
Nothing unapproved is findableSearch is a new way into the same data, and a new way in is exactly where an access rule that was right yesterday quietly stops being right. It gets tested the way the private documents were tested.

Quality checking — a different kind from last time

The checks behind Milestones 2 and 3 confirmed that the right things work. These will confirm that the wrong things are prevented, which is not the same exercise and does not find the same problems.

The two rough edges I wrote to you about on 13 September are the evidence for that. Nineteen checks passed cleanly in August, and the biologist account can still press a button that would pull an approved profile off the public page. That is the class of defect this work exists to catch: not something broken, something insufficiently forbidden.

Realistic pilot data

Search across six profiles with near-identical test data proves nothing — every result looks the same and a filter has nothing to distinguish. Before the search can be shown to anyone, the registry needs a seed set with plausible names, real credential types, and experience genuinely spread across the five domains, so that changing a filter visibly changes the answer.

Documentation, including the guide you asked for

You asked on 13 August to learn to operate the system yourself. I deferred it deliberately, because teaching a workflow that had no reviewer behind it would have gone stale within weeks. The reviewer side now exists, so it belongs here.

DocumentFor
Operator guide — how to grant a reviewer, run a review, handle a rejection, and wake the system when it sleepsYou
Participant guide — how a biologist submitsThe first pilot users
A written check record, the same format as the previous two milestonesThe acceptance record
A short walkthrough video series, recorded once the above is stableYou

The minimum to put it in front of real people

One gap matters more than it sounds: a rejected submission currently cannot be corrected and resubmitted. For a demo that was irrelevant. For a pilot it is not — a pilot where a rejected person is stuck forever is not a pilot. New accounts also need to be able to sign up without me confirming them by hand.

4. What Milestone 5 is not

Stated plainly, because this is the last milestone and the edges are where scope quietly expands. Each of these is real work and worth doing. None is part of the five-milestone schedule, and you separated them yourself in your acceptance note.

Not includedWhy
Deploying to biocredregistry.comProduction is its own piece of work and its own decision.
Production hardening — rate limits, real file-content checking, backups, key rotationThe standing language since May has been “requires production hardening”, and it still applies.
Payments and StripeThe agreed path is unpaid validation first, then a manual pilot, then payments. Unchanged.
Onboarding the general publicA private pilot is a handful of known people. A launch is a different thing with different obligations.

5. One question I need answered, and one I do not

The one I need: who is the search for?

If it is…Then the search is built around…
An agency looking up biologistsDomain, region and credential. Results laid out for comparing candidates.
Biologists finding each otherSpecies, habitat and project type. Results laid out for making contact.

These are different products behind the same search box. Your answer changes the defaults and the result card, not the foundations — so it does not hold me up, but the sooner it lands the less I build twice. If I hear nothing, I will build for the agency case, because that is where the only real interest this project has had came from: your supervisor, on 5 August.

The one I do not need an answer to, but do need started: the Conversion Agreement has been in draft awaiting your legal and tax review since 25 July, and the Functional Pilot Completion document is still blank. Milestone 5 is the one that brings both into play. It would be worth those moving in parallel with the build rather than after it, so that finishing does not then wait on paperwork.
