See it for yourself

A biologist submits their evidence. A reviewer reads it and decides. Only then does a public profile exist. That chain is now built, and this page walks you through it one step at a time.

Nothing to install and nothing to set up. Every step below is a link you click.

Five steps · about 20 minutes · you can stop after step 2 and still have seen the important part

Your two logins

Two of the steps ask you to sign in. I sent both accounts in a separate email — keep it open in another tab as you go.

BiologistThe person applying to the registry. Can submit their own evidence, and nothing else.
ReviewerThe person who decides. Can read submissions and approve or reject them.

Each step below tells you which of the two to use. They are throwaway test accounts — you cannot break anything by clicking around.

Please read before you start

This is a test environment holding made-up data, not the real registry. Please don't sign up your supervisor or anyone else real yet.

It also goes to sleep when unused. If a page ever looks blank or broken, that is almost always why — tell me and I'll wake it up.

  1. 1

    Look at a finished, approved profile

    3 minutes

    This is what the public sees: credentials, experience, and where the person has worked. It is real data from the submission that had been waiting in the queue since 11 August — not a mock-up or a screenshot.

    What to notice: there is nothing here about documents. The evidence behind this profile was read by a reviewer, but it is never shown to the public.
    Don't miss the Experience Map. Top right of the dark bar, the button reading ⬡ View Experience Map. It starts closed on purpose: you read the credentials and the records first, the way you would read anyone's file, and only then turn on the map as an extra layer. Read the page once without it, then press it and read the same page again — the difference between those two readings is the point of the product.
    Why the name looks odd. It reads "M2 Smoke Biologist", and the entries are named things like "Survey 1" — because this is a test environment and that was a test account. The made-up part is the wording. What is real is the path: every field on this page was entered by a user, read by a reviewer, approved, and then published by the system. Nothing here was typed into the page by me.

  2. 2

    Now check that an unapproved profile stays hidden

    1 minute

    There is a profile in the system that nobody has approved. Try to open it the same way you just opened the first one. The registry ID is PEND01.

    What to notice: it refuses. This is the single most important click on the page. Anyone can build a page that shows a profile; a registry is only worth something if a profile nobody vouched for cannot appear. If this one is sitting in the reviewer queue, leave it — approving it would take the check away.
    Try to open it
  3. 3

    Sit in the reviewer's chair

    8 minutes

    Sign in and you get the queue of people waiting to be reviewed. Open one, read what they submitted, open one of their documents, and then approve or reject it.

    If you reject, you have to say why — the system will not let you refuse someone without a stated reason.

    What to notice: underneath each submission there is a record of who decided what, when, and why. That record can be added to, but it can never be edited or deleted afterwards — not by a reviewer, and not by me. And the documents open through a link that stops working after five minutes, so a link forwarded to the wrong person is worthless the next morning.
    Open the reviewer sign-in Use the reviewer account
  4. 4

    Try to do the reviewer's job as a biologist

    2 minutes

    Sign out, sign back in with the biologist account, and then open that same reviewer link from step 3.

    What to notice: you get a locked gate and no data at all. Not a greyed-out button — nothing loads, because the rule lives in the database rather than in the page. Hiding a button is a courtesy; this holds even against someone who skips the web page entirely. There is also no way to make yourself a reviewer from any screen: I grant that by hand.
    Sign in again, as the biologist Use the biologist account
  5. 5

    Optional — walk the whole loop yourself

    10 minutes

    As the biologist, fill in a profile from scratch: add your credentials, add three pieces of experience, upload any PDF you have lying around, and submit it. Then sign back in as the reviewer, approve it, and watch it appear publicly.

    What to notice: the submit button stays locked until the requirements are met — and if you were to get around that button, the database would still refuse the submission. This is the complete product in one sitting.
