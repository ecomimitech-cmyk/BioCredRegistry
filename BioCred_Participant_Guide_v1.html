Submitting your profile

How a biologist creates an account, files evidence, and sends it for review. Written for the staging pilot. The public page only exists after a reviewer has read the file and approved it.

This environment is a test. Use documents you are willing to put on a staging system. Do not treat a profile here as a public citation until you are told it has moved to the production registry.

1. What you are submitting

BioCred is a record of documentation, not a rating of you. A reviewer reads what you filed and decides whether it supports what you claimed. An approval means the documents were read and are consistent with the profile. It does not mean the registry has ranked you against anyone else.

Three things have to be on the file before it can be sent:

RequirementWhat counts
At least one identity credentialA degree, a professional licence, or a professional certification, with the document attached.
At least three experience recordsSeparate pieces of work, each filed under one of the five domains.
At least one stored documentA file actually uploaded, not only a title typed in.

The page shows these three counts as you go. When all three are met, the send button unlocks.

2. Create an account

  1. Open the biologist sign-in page and choose Create account.
  2. Use an email you can read, and a password of at least eight characters.
  3. If the page says the address must be confirmed before you can sign in, wait for that message and follow it. If nothing arrives, tell the person who invited you — on staging, confirming an address is sometimes done by hand.
  4. Sign in. You land on the evidence intake page. The address in the corner is the account the profile will belong to.
One account, one profile. There is no way to file a profile for someone else from this page.

3. Save the draft profile

The first card is the draft. Nothing else on the page works until this is saved.

  1. Fill in your name. Title, organisation, discipline, city, state, years, and a short summary are what a reader sees later, so write them as you would want them cited.
  2. Accept the staging terms. The box disappears once it has been accepted.
  3. Press Save draft profile. The page then shows a registry id, which is the public identifier of this file. Keep it. After approval, that id is how someone opens your profile directly.

You can come back and press Update draft profile at any time before a reviewer has the file. After you send it, wait for the decision before changing it.

4. Add documents

  1. Choose a category. For the identity requirement, use Degree Certificate / Diploma, Professional Certification, or Professional License.
  2. Give the document its real title and, where you have it, the issuing authority and the issue date.
  3. Attach the file. PDF, JPG, or PNG, up to 10 MB.
  4. Tick the applicant attestation that the information is true, accurate, and complete, then press Save document.
  5. Repeat for each further document. A transcript, a permit, continuing education, a reference, or a CV can sit alongside the identity credential. They do not replace it.
The file is private. It is not placed on the public profile. A reviewer opens it through a link that stops working after five minutes. Do not send anyone a copy of that link — it will already be dead, and the document was never meant to travel that way.

5. Add experience

Each record is one piece of work, not a whole career in a single box. You need at least three.

  1. Pick the domain that the work actually sits in: field experience, regulatory experience, academic / technical, species depth, or leadership scope.
  2. Name the activity, and add the species or system and the years where they apply.
  3. Set the role you held on that work.
  4. Press Add experience record. The count on the submit card goes up by one.

These records are what later draw the experience map. The map is a picture of where the documented work sits. It is not a score, and nothing you type here is turned into one.

6. Send it for review

The last card is Submit for review. It stays locked until the three requirements in section 1 are met. The pills on that card say which one is still short.

  1. Read the pills. A warning colour means that item is still missing.
  2. Press Submit profile for review.
  3. The status becomes pending. The button locks. A reviewer now has the file, and there is nothing further to do until they decide.
While it is pending, or while a reviewer has started on it, you cannot send it again. That is deliberate. Sending it again would pull an approved profile off the public registry, so the same rule applies before approval too.

7. If it comes back

A rejection is a request to correct the file, not a closed door. Sign in again and open the intake page. A box at the bottom quotes the reviewer's reason. That sentence is the instruction. Fix the specific document or the specific claim it names.

  1. Update the draft, replace the document, or add the experience record the reason asked for.
  2. In What have you changed since?, write what you actually changed. The reviewer reads that note beside the file. The page will not send the correction without it, and neither will the database.
  3. Press Resubmit corrected profile.

The reviewer sees that this is a second look before opening it, and both sides of the exchange stay in the decision history: their reason, then your note.

8. After it is approved

You will be told when the profile is public. It then has a stable address built from the registry id, and it can be found from the search page by name, region, discipline, or the domain the experience sits in.

On the public profile, the experience map starts closed. It is a layer on top of the credentials and the records, switched on with View Experience Map. The same shape appears on the search card, at the same size for every person. A spike means the documented work concentrates in one domain. An even pentagon means it is spread. Neither means better.

Once the profile is approved, Submit profile for review stays off. Pressing it is refused. A new submission would take the profile off the public registry until someone approved it again.

9. What you will not be asked to do

You do thisYou do not do this
Create your own account, file your own evidence, and answer a rejection in your own words. Approve anything. There is no path from this account to the review queue. Reviewer access is granted separately, and never from the sign-up page.
Read your own decision history, including the reason you were turned down. See anyone else's submission, documents, or decision.
Wait, if the first page after a quiet week hangs. Staging sleeps. Reload after a minute. Pay anything. The pilot does not take payment.
